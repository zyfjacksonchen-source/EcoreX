# Xiaohongshu Native Copy Rules

Use this for titles, body copy, tags, and first comments.

## Titles

- Every title candidate and selected title must be 20 characters or fewer.
- Count Chinese characters, Latin letters, numbers, punctuation, and emoji as characters.
- If a good title is too long, shorten it before showing it.
- Prefer one strong hook over a complete sentence.
- Use at most one emoji in a title, and only when it improves native feel without wasting characters.

Good title patterns:

- `福建装修先问工人`
- `装修别只看报价`
- `自有工人有多重要`
- `装修怕转包看这篇`

## Body Rhythm

Write like a useful Xiaohongshu note, not a brochure:

- Open with a concrete user anxiety or scene.
- Use short paragraphs, usually 1-3 lines.
- Mix explanation, checklist, and soft personal judgement.
- Insert natural emoji as visual anchors, especially before checklists and CTA.
- Do not put emoji on every line; keep density human.
- Avoid long corporate claims. Translate selling points into user benefits.

Recommended emoji density for a medium note:

- Title: 0-1.
- Body: 4-10 total.
- First comment: 0-1.
- Tags: no emoji inside tags.

## CTA

Use a clear low-friction action:

- `私信【区域+面积】`
- `评论/私信发户型图`
- `想先看报价，可以发我小区+面积`

Do not overpromise. Keep CTA aligned to the actual client offer.

## Final Copy Check

Before delivery, verify:

- All titles are <=20 characters.
- Body has native rhythm and natural emoji.
- CTA is clear and low friction.
- Claims are supported by the brief or softened.
